Authoritative Guide to Brick & Block Masonry Quantity Takeoffs

Accurate material estimation is the bedrock of cost management in masonry construction, whether building a residential perimeter garden wall, a multi-story commercial veneer cavity system, or an industrial retaining wall using reinforced Concrete Masonry Units (CMU). Under-ordering bricks delays project timelines and risks noticeable batch color mismatches from separate kiln firings. Conversely, over-ordering ties up working capital in non-returnable pallets. Governed by standards from the Masonry Institute of America (MIA), ASTM C216 (Facing Brick), and ASTM C90 (Loadbearing CMU), professional estimating involves precise geometric calculations factoring in mortar bed joint thicknesses, wall wythe layers, and cutting allowances.

Nominal vs Specified Masonry Dimensions

In architectural drawings and specifications, a critical distinction exists between specified (manufactured) dimensions and nominal dimensions. The nominal dimension always includes the thickness of one standard mortar joint—typically 3/8 inch (9.5 mm) in US practice, or 10 mm in European and British standards. For example:

  • Standard US Modular Brick: Actual manufactured dimensions are $3\frac{5}{8} \times 2\frac{1}{4} \times 7\frac{5}{8}$ inches. When laid with a $3/8$-inch mortar joint, its nominal modular size becomes $4 \times 2\frac{2}{3} \times 8$ inches ($102 \times 68 \times 203$ mm). Three courses of modular brick equal exactly 8 inches in vertical height, perfectly matching standard concrete block modular grid coordination!
  • Standard 8-Inch CMU Block: Actual manufactured dimensions are $7\frac{5}{8} \times 7\frac{5}{8} \times 15\frac{5}{8}$ inches. With the 3/8-inch bed joint and head joint, nominal dimensions are exactly $8 \times 8 \times 16$ inches ($200 \times 200 \times 400$ mm).

Mathematical Governing Equations

The net surface area ($A_{net}$) of the wall to be laid is the gross wall area minus the area of all window, door, and architectural openings:

$$A_{net} = (L_{wall} \times H_{wall}) - \sum A_{openings}$$

The face area occupied by a single masonry unit ($A_{unit}$) including its mortar joints is:

$$A_{unit} = (L_{unit} + t_{joint}) \times (H_{unit} + t_{joint})$$

For standard US Modular brick in imperial units:

$$A_{unit} = \left( 7.625 + 0.375 \right) \text{ in} \times \left( 2.25 + 0.375 \right) \text{ in} = 8.0 \text{ in} \times 2.625 \text{ in} = 21.0 \text{ sq inches} = 0.14583 \text{ sq ft}$$

The number of bricks per square foot of wall surface is:

$$\text{Bricks per sq ft} = \frac{1}{0.14583} \approx 6.857 \text{ bricks/sq ft (approx. 6.75 to 7.0)}$$

Multiplying by the wall layer multiplier ($W = 1$ for single wythe, $W = 2$ for double wythe) and adding the cutting and breakage waste factor ($f_{waste} \approx 0.10$ for 10%):

$$\text{Total Bricks} = A_{net} \times (\text{Bricks per sq ft}) \times W \times (1 + f_{waste})$$

Mortar Estimation & Mix Ratios (ASTM C270)

Mortar accounts for approximately 15% to 20% of the total volume of a completed brick wall. Under ASTM C270, masonry mortars are categorized by compressive strength:

  • Type M (2,500 psi / 17.2 MPa): High compressive strength; recommended for below-grade foundation retaining walls, heavy loadbearing piers, and severe frost exposure.
  • Type S (1,800 psi / 12.4 MPa): High flexural bond strength; ideal for seismic zones, exterior walls subject to high lateral wind loads, and reinforced CMU masonry.
  • Type N (750 psi / 5.2 MPa): General-purpose medium strength; the industry standard for above-grade exterior residential brick veneer and interior partitions.

On average, an 80 lb (36.3 kg) bag of pre-mixed Type N masonry cement combined with clean masonry sand yields approximately 0.75 cubic feet of wet mortar. In field practice, contractors estimate:

$$\text{Mortar Bags} = \frac{\text{Total Bricks}}{30 \text{ to } 35 \text{ bricks per bag}} \quad \text{or for CMU: } \frac{\text{Total Blocks}}{12 \text{ to } 14 \text{ blocks per bag}}$$

Worked Practical Example: Residential Garage Brick Veneer

Real-World Contractor Quantity Takeoff:

Design Scenario: A masonry contractor is installing a single-wythe exterior facing brick veneer on a residential garage wall. The design parameters are:

  • Wall Dimensions: 30 feet long × 9 feet high
  • Openings: 1 garage door (8 ft × 7 ft = 56 sq ft) and 1 pedestrian door (3 ft × 7 ft = 21 sq ft)
  • Brick Specification: Standard US Modular brick (6.75 bricks/sq ft)
  • Wastage Allowance: 10% cutting and chipping margin

Step 1: Calculate Gross and Net Wall Areas:

$$A_{gross} = 30 \text{ ft} \times 9 \text{ ft} = 270.0 \text{ sq ft}$$

$$A_{openings} = 56 + 21 = 77.0 \text{ sq ft}$$

$$A_{net} = 270.0 - 77.0 = 193.0 \text{ sq ft}$$

Step 2: Calculate Base Brick Count:

$$\text{Base Bricks} = 193.0 \text{ sq ft} \times 6.75 \text{ bricks/sq ft} = 1,302.75 \approx 1,303 \text{ bricks}$$

Step 3: Factor 10% Waste Margin:

$$\text{Total Order} = 1,303 \times 1.10 = 1,433.3 \rightarrow \mathbf{1,434 \text{ Modular Bricks (3 pallets of 500)}}$$

Step 4: Calculate Mortar & Sand Requirements:

$$\text{Mortar Bags (80 lb)} = \frac{1,434}{33} \approx \mathbf{44 \text{ Bags of Type N Mortar}}$$

$$\text{Masonry Sand} = 44 \text{ bags} \times 0.035 \text{ tons/bag} \approx \mathbf{1.54 \text{ Tons of Sand}}$$

Frequently Asked Questions About Brick Takeoffs

How many standard modular bricks are required per square foot of wall?
For standard US modular facing bricks (3-5/8 x 2-1/4 x 7-5/8 inches) laid with a standard 3/8-inch mortar joint, exactly 6.75 bricks are required per square foot of single-wythe wall surface (or approximately 7.4 bricks per sq ft when factoring in a standard 10% cutting and breakage allowance).
How many concrete blocks (CMU) are required per 100 square feet of wall?
A standard 8x8x16 concrete masonry unit (nominal dimensions including 3/8-inch mortar joint) has a nominal face area of 8 x 16 inches = 128 sq in (0.888 sq ft). Exactly 112.5 blocks are required per 100 square feet of wall surface (approx 124 blocks with 10% waste).
How many bags of masonry mortar do I need per 100 bricks?
On average, one 80 lb (36 kg) bag of Type N or Type S pre-blended masonry cement mortar will lay approximately 30 to 35 standard modular bricks or 12 to 14 standard 8-inch CMU blocks. Therefore, 100 bricks require roughly 3 bags of mortar.
What is the difference between single-wythe and double-wythe brick walls?
A single-wythe wall consists of a single layer of bricks in thickness (typically used as an exterior veneer over wood or steel framing). A double-wythe wall has two parallel layers of bricks bonded together with mortar headers or metal ties, requiring exactly double the quantity of bricks.
