💡 Direct Answer (GEO & Quick Summary)

Cantilever retaining wall geotechnical stability is governed by Rankine active earth pressure:

Ka = (1 - sin φ) ÷ (1 + sin φ) = tan²(45° - φ/2)

Total horizontal thrust is Pa = 0.5 × Ka × γs × H_total² acting at $H/3$ above the base. Safety factors must satisfy:

  • Overturning: FS_overturn = Σ M_resisting ÷ Σ M_overturning ≥ 1.5 to 2.0
  • Sliding: FS_sliding = (μ × Σ V) ÷ Pa ≥ 1.5
  • Bearing Capacity: Maximum toe pressure qmax ≤ qa with resultant eccentricity within the kern (e ≤ B/6).

Rankine Active Earth Pressure Theory

A retaining wall is a vital civil engineering structure engineered to retain soil, rock, or granular backfill material at a vertical or near-vertical slope where natural topography precludes unconfined embankment grading. When the backfill is placed behind the wall, the soil mass exerts horizontal lateral earth pressures. If the wall is permitted to yield slightly outward away from the backfill (typically by an angular rotation of merely 0.001 to 0.002 radians), shear resistance develops within the soil mass along an internal planar failure wedge, reducing lateral pressure to its minimum limit state: Rankine Active Earth Pressure.

Scottish engineer William John Macquorn Rankine formulated the classical mathematical relationship for frictionless, level backfills:

1. Rankine Active Earth Pressure Coefficient (Ka)
K_a = \tan^2\left(45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}\right) = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi}
Where $\phi$ is the internal angle of friction of the granular backfill soil (typically 28° to 36° for cohesionless sands and gravels).
2. Total Lateral Active Soil Thrust (Pa)
P_a = \frac{1}{2} K_a \times \gamma_s \times H_{\text{total}}^2 \quad (\text{Acting at lever arm } y = H_{\text{total}} / 3)
Where $\gamma_s$ is soil unit weight and $H_{\text{total}} = H_{\text{stem}} + D_{\text{footing}}$ is total height from foundation base.

The Three Pillars of Geotechnical Stability

Before undertaking structural reinforcement detailing under ACI 318, the civil structural engineer must verify that the overall physical geometry of the wall satisfies three independent external limit states under unfactored service loads:

1. Factor of Safety Against Overturning (FS_overturn)
FS_{\text{overturn}} = \frac{\sum M_R}{\sum M_O} \ge 1.50 \quad (\text{or } 2.0\text{ under AASHTO highway design})
Where $\sum M_R$ is the sum of resisting moments about the toe (stem weight + footing weight + soil heel weight), and $\sum M_O = P_a \times (H / 3)$ is the overturning moment.
2. Factor of Safety Against Base Sliding (FS_slide)
FS_{\text{slide}} = \frac{\mu \times \sum V + P_p}{P_a} \ge 1.50
Where $\mu = \tan\delta$ is the base soil-concrete friction coefficient, $\sum V$ is total vertical dead load, and $P_p$ is passive soil resistance (frequently neglected for conservatism).
3. Foundation Soil Bearing Capacity Pressures (qmax & qmin)
q_{\max, \min} = \frac{\sum V}{B} \left( 1 \pm \frac{6 e}{B} \right) \le q_{\text{allowable}}
Where $e$ is resultant load eccentricity from base center. To prevent tensile soil separation at the heel, $q_{\min} \ge 0$ requires $e \le B / 6$.

The Middle-Third Rule & Resultant Eccentricity

Soil exhibits high compressive bearing strength but virtually zero tensile capacity. If the resultant vertical line of action shifts excessively toward the forward toe due to lateral overturning thrust, the heel of the foundation slab attempts to lift off the subgrade, concentrating destructive peak bearing stresses along the forward toe edge.

To eliminate subgrade separation, classical soil mechanics enforces the Middle-Third Rule: the eccentricity $e$ of the resultant force must fall within the central third of the foundation slab base:

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum M_R - \sum M_O}{\sum V}, \quad e = \frac{B}{2} - \bar{x} \le \frac{B}{6} \]

When $e \le B/6$, the contact stress profile is trapezoidal and entirely compressive ($q_{\min} > 0$). If $e > B/6$, triangular stress redistribution occurs ($q_{\max} = \frac{2 \sum V}{3 \bar{x}}$), which severely concentrates stresses and risks progressive subgrade bearing shear failure.

📐 Comprehensive Worked Case Study: 4.5m Cantilever Retaining Wall Stability Analysis

Geotechnical Stability Case Study
Step 1: Compute Rankine Active Thrust & Overturning Moment
\[ H_{\text{tot}} = 4.50\text{ m},\quad \phi = 32^\circ \implies K_a = \frac{1 - \sin 32^\circ}{1 + \sin 32^\circ} = \frac{0.4701}{1.5299} = 0.3073 \] \[ P_a = \frac{1}{2} (0.3073) (18.5) (4.50)^2 = 57.55\text{ kN/m} \quad (\text{Arm } y = 1.50\text{ m}) \] \[ M_O = 57.55 \times 1.50 = 86.33\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m/m} \]

Total lateral active thrust equals 57.55 kN per linear meter of wall, yielding an overturning moment of 86.33 kN·m/m.

Step 2: Resolve Component Weights & Resisting Moments About Toe
\[ W_1\ (\text{Stem Concrete } 0.4 \times 4.0 \times 24) = 38.4\text{ kN/m},\quad \text{Arm } = 0.90\text{ m} \implies M_1 = 34.56 \] \[ W_2\ (\text{Base Concrete } 2.8 \times 0.5 \times 24) = 33.6\text{ kN/m},\quad \text{Arm } = 1.40\text{ m} \implies M_2 = 47.04 \] \[ W_3\ (\text{Soil Heel } 1.7 \times 4.0 \times 18.5) = 125.8\text{ kN/m},\quad \text{Arm } = 1.95\text{ m} \implies M_3 = 245.31 \] \[ \sum V = 197.8\text{ kN/m},\quad \sum M_R = 34.56 + 47.04 + 245.31 = 326.91\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m/m} \]

The heavy soil prism resting atop the heel provides over 75% of total resisting moment!

Step 3: Evaluate Overturning & Sliding Safety Factors
\[ FS_{\text{overturn}} = \frac{326.91}{86.33} = 3.79 \ge 1.50 \quad (\textbf{PASS}) \] \[ FS_{\text{slide}} = \frac{\mu \times \sum V}{P_a} = \frac{0.48 \times 197.8}{57.55} = \frac{94.94}{57.55} = 1.65 \ge 1.50 \quad (\textbf{PASS}) \]

Both factors of safety exceed the 1.50 statutory limit without requiring an anchor shear key.

Step 4: Check Resultant Eccentricity & Soil Bearing Pressure
\[ \bar{x} = \frac{326.91 - 86.33}{197.8} = 1.216\text{ m},\quad e = \frac{2.8}{2} - 1.216 = 0.184\text{ m} \le \frac{2.8}{6} = 0.467\text{ m} \] \[ q_{\max} = \frac{197.8}{2.8} \left( 1 + \frac{6(0.184)}{2.8} \right) = 70.64 \times (1 + 0.394) = 98.47\text{ kPa} \le 180\text{ kPa} \quad (\textbf{PASS}) \]

Resultant falls well within the kern; maximum toe bearing stress of 98.5 kPa is safely below allowable capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions (Retaining Wall Design)

What is the difference between active (Ka), at-rest (K0), and passive (Kp) earth pressure?
Active Earth Pressure ($K_a$) occurs when the retaining wall rotates or deflects slightly outward away from the backfill, allowing the soil to expand and mobilize shear strength along a slip plane (minimum lateral force). At-Rest Earth Pressure ($K_0 \approx 1 - \sin\phi$) occurs when the wall is rigidly unyielding (such as a basement wall braced by floor diaphragms), yielding 40% to 50% higher lateral pressure. Passive Earth Pressure ($K_p = 1 / K_a$) occurs when the wall is forced into the soil mass, developing enormous resisting thrust (maximum lateral resistance).
What is a shear key and when is it necessary on a cantilever retaining wall?
A shear key is a downward reinforced concrete projection constructed beneath the base slab footing into the firm underlying subgrade. When the factor of safety against sliding ($FS_{\text{slide}}$) is below 1.50 using base friction alone, the shear key mobilizes passive soil resistance ($P_p$) and forces the potential sliding failure plane through the deeper soil strata, dramatically increasing sliding resistance without requiring an excessively wide footing slab.
Why is backfill drainage critical in preventing retaining wall collapse?
Water trapped behind a retaining wall introduces hydrostatic water pressure ($\gamma_w = 9.81\text{ kN/m}^3$ or $62.4\text{ pcf}$) in addition to buoyant lateral soil pressure. Hydrostatic pressure acts with a coefficient of 1.0 (compared to $K_a \approx 0.30$ for dry soil), more than tripling total overturning thrust! Modern walls must incorporate perforated drain pipes, geocomposite drainage boards, crushed gravel drainage blankets, and weep holes through the stem to prevent hydrostatic water accumulation.
How does surface surcharge load (traffic or buildings) affect retaining wall thrust?
A surface surcharge load $q$ (such as vehicle parking, highway traffic, or an adjacent building foundation) exerts a uniform lateral pressure $\Delta p = K_a \cdot q$ along the entire vertical height of the wall. Unlike triangular soil pressure (which acts at $H/3$), rectangular surcharge pressure produces a horizontal resultant force $P_s = K_a \cdot q \cdot H$ acting at mid-height ($H/2$), significantly amplifying overturning moments.
