Peak Sun Hours vs. Daylight Hours

A "Peak Sun Hour" (PSh) does not simply equal daylight duration. One peak sun hour corresponds to solar irradiance of 1,000 Watts per square meter (1 kW/m²). A location receiving 12 hours of sunshine may only accumulate 4.5 Peak Sun Hours due to morning and evening sun angle attenuation.

Photovoltaic Array Sizing Formula
PV Size (kW) = (Daily Energy Demand in kWh · 1.25) / Peak Sun Hours
1.25 factor accounts for dust, wiring voltage drop, and inverter DC-to-AC losses

Accounting for Real-World Losses

Solar arrays never operate at 100% laboratory Standard Test Conditions (STC). In field installations, efficiency diminishes due to thermal coefficient losses (silicon panels lose ~0.4% efficiency for every degree above 25°C), inverter DC-to-AC conversion (typically 95-97%), and dust accumulation.

📐 Field Case Study: 30 kWh/Day Residential Off-Grid Solar Array Sizing

Real-World Renewable Energy Problem
Step 1: Quantify Daily Energy Target with System Inefficiency Compensation
\[ E_{target} = \frac{E_{load}}{\eta_{sys}} = \frac{30\text{ kWh}}{0.80} = 37.5\text{ kWh/day} \]

To reliably supply 30 kWh of daily household consumption with 20% system losses, the PV array must generate 37.5 kWh daily.

Step 2: Size Solar Array Peak Wattage (Wp) Based on Peak Sun Hours (PSH)
\[ P_{pv} = \frac{E_{target}}{\text{PSH}} = \frac{37{,}500\text{ Wh}}{4.8\text{ PSH}} = 7{,}812.5\text{ W} \approx 8.0\text{ kWp} \]

With 4.8 Peak Sun Hours of winter solar insolation, the required solar peak capacity is approximately 8.0 kWp.

Step 3: Determine Module Count for 550W Tier-1 Panels
\[ \text{Modules} = \lceil \frac{7{,}812.5\text{ W}}{550\text{ W}} \rceil = 15\text{ Panels} \implies 8{,}250\text{ W total} \]

Fifteen 550W mono-PERC modules deliver 8,250 Watts peak (8.25 kWp). Size your battery storage with our Solar Battery Bank Calculator.

Step 4: Size Matching Off-Grid Inverter and DC Cabling
\[ P_{inv} \ge 8.0\text{ kW},\quad I_{dc} = \frac{8{,}250\text{ W}}{48\text{ V}} = 171.9\text{ A} \]

Solar Photovoltaic Resource Geometry & Array Sizing Formulations

Dimensioning a solar photovoltaic array involves balancing building electrical load demand against geographical solar irradiance. Array sizing calculations combine solar astronomical geometry, local Peak Sun Hours (PSH), array tilt transposition, and system loss derating factors defined in NREL PVWatts methodologies.

Peak Sun Hours (PSH) & Global Horizontal Irradiance Integration

Solar irradiance measures instantaneous solar radiant power flux per unit area ($\text{W/m}^2$). Solar irradiation, or insolation, represents cumulative energy integrated over time ($\text{kWh/m}^2/\text{day}$). One Peak Sun Hour (PSH) is defined as the equivalent duration that solar irradiance would need to shine at standardized STC intensity ($1{,}000\text{ W/m}^2$) to deliver identical total daily insolation:

$$1\text{ PSH} \equiv 1.0\text{ kWh/m}^2/\text{day} = 3.6\text{ MJ/m}^2/\text{day}$$

Total Array Capacity Sizing Formulation

The total DC nameplate array capacity ($P_{\text{array}}$ in kilowatts-peak, $\text{kWp}$) required to offset average daily energy consumption ($E_{\text{daily}}$ in $\text{kWh/day}$) is calculated as:

$$P_{\text{array}}(\text{kWp}) = \frac{E_{\text{daily}}(\text{kWh/day})}{\text{PSH} \times \eta_{\text{system}}}$$ $$N_{\text{panels}} = \left\lceil \frac{P_{\text{array}} \times 1000}{P_{\text{module,STC}}} \right\rceil$$

Where $\eta_{\text{system}}$ represents the compound system derating factor ($\approx 75\% - 82\%$), combining soiling ($2\% - 5\%$), thermal cell heat derating ($8\% - 14\%$), wiring ohmic drop ($2\%$), inverter conversion losses ($2\% - 4\%$), and module nameplate tolerance mismatch ($1\%$).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Peak Sun Hours (PSH) and how are they measured?
Peak Sun Hours (PSH) represent the equivalent number of hours in a day during which solar irradiance equals 1,000 W/m² (Standard Test Conditions). A location with 12 hours of sunlight might receive only 4.5 to 5.5 PSH due to low morning and evening sun angles.
How do hot summer temperatures impact solar panel power output?
Solar panels lose efficiency as cell temperature rises above 25°C. Silicon panels have a temperature coefficient of Pmax of approximately -0.35% to -0.40% per °C. On a 40°C day with cell temperatures reaching 65°C, output drops by roughly 14% to 16%.
What is the difference between Monocrystalline and Polycrystalline panels?
Monocrystalline panels are sliced from single-crystal silicon ingots, delivering higher efficiency (20% to 22%), superior low-light performance, and sleek black aesthetics. Polycrystalline panels are made from melted silicon fragments, with lower efficiency (15% to 17%) and a speckled blue appearance.
What tilt angle is optimal for fixed solar panels?
A common rule of thumb for fixed year-round solar panels is to set the tilt angle equal to the local latitude, facing true South (Northern Hemisphere) or true North (Southern Hemisphere). For winter-optimized off-grid systems, tilt is increased by 10° to 15° to capture low winter sun.
