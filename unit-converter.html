Scientific & Mathematical Fundamentals

By CalcHub Universal Unit Converters Editorial Board • Verified against NIST & BIPM SI Units
Executive Summary: Dimensional analysis and unit conversion translate physical quantities between the International System of Units (SI) and US Customary / British Imperial measurement systems.

1. Engineering Principles & Definitions

Scientific and engineering calculations require dimensional homogeneity. In 1959, the International Yard and Pound Agreement formally standardized the conversion ratio between imperial and metric systems: defining exactly 1 inch = 25.4 millimeters and 1 avoirdupois pound = 0.45359237 kilograms. Failure to convert units accurately has precipitated notorious engineering disasters, including the loss of NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999.

2. Mathematical Formulas & Governing Equations

Linear conversions use fixed multiplication factors, while affine conversions (temperature) incorporate zero-point offsets:

\[ \text{Value}_{target} = \text{Value}_{source} \times \left(\frac{K_{source}}{K_{target}}\right) \]\[ T_{^{\circ}\text{F}} = (T_{^{\circ}\text{C}} \times 1.8) + 32 \quad \vert \quad T_{\text{K}} = T_{^{\circ}\text{C}} + 273.15 \]

4. Worked Real-World Engineering Example

Converting a vehicle tire pressure of 2.20 bar into US Customary pounds per square inch (psi). One bar equals 100,000 Pascals; one psi equals 6,894.76 Pascals. Therefore, 2.20 bar = 220,000 Pa. Dividing by 6,894.76 gives 31.91 psi.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between mass and weight?
Mass (measured in kilograms) is an intrinsic property representing the amount of matter in an object, remaining identical anywhere in the universe. Weight is the gravitational force acting on that mass (W = m × g), varying with local gravitational acceleration.
Why is -40° identical in Celsius and Fahrenheit?
Because the two linear temperature scale formulas intersect at: -40 × 1.8 + 32 = -72 + 32 = -40°.
What is the difference between US liquid gallon and UK Imperial gallon?
A US liquid gallon equals 231 cubic inches (3.785 liters), whereas an Imperial gallon equals 4.546 liters (approximately 20% larger).
